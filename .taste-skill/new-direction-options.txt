# 3 directions dealt for this brief

Choose the one whose way of composing can best carry an idea from your inventory (SKILL.md steps 1 and 4), not the
most familiar one. Then deal it in full with the same command plus `--direction <id>` (and without --options).

## `page-is-the-object` — The page is the object
Idea engine: Find the printed or physical artefact that already exists in the subject's world and build the page as that thing. A wedding site is the invitation card.
Composition: The first screen shows the artefact as a single filled panel (paper colour on a contrasting ground) centred or offset, with believable proportions: a card at 5:7, a ticket at about 2.2:1 with a notch cut by `clip-path`, a menu as a tall sheet. All primary content lives on it, set the way that object is set: centred and symmetrical for an invitation, in tidy aligned columns for a menu or ticket.

## `sentence-as-layout` — The sentence is the layout
Idea engine: One short sentence, set at poster scale, is the composition. The trick that makes it an idea rather than a big headline: something from the subject sits inside the sentence where a word would be.
Composition: The sentence is four to seven words and runs as one or two full-width lines at 9–13vw. Inline with the type, on the baseline, sits one media slot: a rounded-rectangle or square frame exactly one line-height tall and two to three ems wide, holding a photograph or a looping crop.

## `type-only-statement` — Type and nothing else
Idea engine: The first screen is made only of words, colour and space, good enough that nobody misses a picture there. Pictures arrive later, large and few, as relief from the type.
Composition: First screen: the statement, two lines at most, set at 9–14vw and occupying the full width with tight leading, positioned in the upper two-thirds. One supporting sentence at 20–24px sits bottom-left on the same margin and the single action sits bottom-right on the same baseline.
